# 多略微信公众号头图 Brand Rules

## 1. Core visual values
多略头图 should express:
- **观点先于装饰** — the cover must take a position or show why the event matters.
- **信息密度高，但秩序更高** — dense is acceptable; chaotic is not.
- **技术感必须服务理解** — UI, charts, nodes, code, grids, arrows, glow are explanatory devices, not wallpaper.
- **品牌识别即信息** — company/model colors, marks, product shapes, and people can carry meaning faster than extra copy.
- **商业媒体感 > 艺术海报感 > AI梦幻感**.
- **真实世界锚点 > 抽象AI符号** whenever a real person, product, company, policy venue, device, or evidence object exists.

## 2. Observed preferred aesthetic
Default visual personality:
- dark, high-contrast, premium, editorial;
- black / near-black / deep navy as common base;
- white / warm white as headline color;
- one or two brand-derived accent colors;
- cinematic lighting and depth;
- bold Chinese typography with English model/product names integrated naturally;
- clear foreground / midground / background separation;
- large composited portraits or large product identity anchors;
- restrained glow, neon edge, HUD, grid, chart, or data texture when relevant.

This is NOT a rigid dark-mode requirement. Use a light background when the content itself calls for paper, documents, education, diagrams, or another grounded material; preserve strong contrast and editorial force.

## 3. Headline behavior
Preferred:
- headline often occupies **35–60% of perceived attention**;
- 1–3 lines;
- oversized, heavy, direct;
- key phrase highlighted in brand color / orange / gold / green / cyan as semantically appropriate;
- Chinese + English product/model names in the same headline are welcome;
- line breaks should amplify rhythm and judgment.

Avoid:
- small centered title floating above a giant illustration;
- thin elegant type that loses on mobile;
- long subtitle paragraphs;
- five different text styles;
- decorative English that says nothing.

## 4. Information hierarchy
Preferred hierarchy:
1. article thesis / headline;
2. dominant face / product / evidence object;
3. explanatory relationship or mechanism;
4. secondary labels / metrics;
5. background texture.

Secondary cards, badges, score blocks, labels, diagrams, and captions are allowed when they explain:
- comparison;
- mechanism;
- direction;
- power relationship;
- benchmark result;
- product split / merge;
- policy / market context;
- evidence / detection.

Remove any secondary layer that does not improve understanding.

## 5. People
When a person matters to the story:
- use a **large editorial portrait**, not a tiny avatar;
- photorealistic / press-photo quality;
- serious, analytical, confident, confrontational, or contemplative expressions are preferred over generic smiling;
- strong cutout, controlled rim light, realistic skin texture;
- compose gaze to support narrative: toward opponent, product, evidence, or future direction;
- keep face identity clean; do not over-stylize.

Typical patterns:
- left/right two-person confrontation;
- two people framing a central system;
- one large person with evidence / policy / product context beside them;
- one dominant person with rivals represented as product cards rather than more faces.

## 6. Brand and product color logic
Use color semantically.
Examples of preferred behavior:
- Claude / Anthropic topics -> warm orange / amber / cream accents;
- OpenAI / Codex -> black, white, cool blue / cyan when contrast is needed;
- NVIDIA -> green as a high-value accent, not a full-screen wash;
- ByteDance / Feishu / Doubao / Volcano -> cool blue / cyan with orange/red separation where the business split requires it;
- Grok / xAI -> black / white / cool electric blue unless the specific asset establishes another identity;
- DeepSeek -> cool blue / cyan identity cues;
- multi-brand comparison -> let each side retain distinct brand identity; use background neutrality to prevent color mud.

Do not blindly color the entire image in one brand color.
Brand color should encode identity, side, direction, or emphasis.

## 7. Preferred composition behaviors
Strongly preferred:
- **thesis + evidence**: huge title + magnifier/document/detection card;
- **binary conflict**: two entities separated by diagonal/energy split, title bridges them;
- **system center**: two people/brands on edges, harness/system/process in center;
- **flow / split**: source product in center-left, arrows branching to destinations;
- **benchmark arena**: winning model/product centered, competitors flanking, 2–3 metric panels below;
- **person + context**: portrait foreground, product/social post/policy building behind or beside;
- **cinematic launch**: product/logo + dramatic cultural/creative scene + large verdict.

Weak defaults:
- generic 3-column cards with equal weight;
- evenly spaced icon grids;
- a single logo centered on an abstract sci-fi background;
- random collage without directional logic.

## 8. Controlled visual density
多略 does NOT require minimalism.
A cover may include:
- 1 large title block;
- 1–2 major entities;
- 1 mechanism / evidence layer;
- 1 short supporting strip;
- 2–3 metrics or capability blocks in benchmark stories.

But enforce:
- one dominant focal point;
- one dominant reading path;
- clear size hierarchy;
- large clean whitespace/negative zones around the headline even inside a dense composition;
- no more than ~3 semantic layers competing at once.

## 9. Technology visual language
Allowed when meaningful:
- terminal/code windows;
- model cards;
- data charts;
- network nodes;
- cloud/server visualization;
- architecture blocks;
- flow arrows;
- forensic overlays;
- subtle grids / circuit traces;
- neon edge-light and glass panels.

Avoid as default filler:
- glowing AI brain;
- humanoid robot head;
- galaxy / nebula;
- random binary rain;
- meaningless hologram sphere;
- excessive lens flares;
- generic cyberpunk city.

## 10. Emotional temperature
Preferred range:
- analytical tension;
- competitive tension;
- discovery;
- strategic power;
- technological acceleration;
- risk / controversy;
- decisive product launch.

Avoid default moods:
- cute;
- cozy;
- dreamy;
- inspirational lifestyle;
- cheerful corporate teamwork;
- soft wellness aesthetics.

## 11. Specific recurring visual values inferred from approved covers
- **The title can be the hero.** Large type is not a fallback; it is a core visual device.
- **A cover should explain a relationship.** “A beats B”, “A splits into B/C”, “A vs B”, “person -> policy”, “evidence -> conclusion”.
- **Use faces when power, leadership, conflict, or authorship matters.** Do not use faces when the mechanism itself is stronger.
- **Use brand colors to segment competing sides.** Contrast between sides is preferred over a blended palette.
- **Use UI/diagram cards as proof-like objects.** They should look legible enough to signal meaning, not imitate a full dashboard.
- **Dramatization is welcome when grounded in the article.** Cinematic lighting, large scale, strong perspective, torn-paper or energy splits can amplify a real thesis.
- **Do not become fantastical.** The drama should still feel like tech/business media, not concept art detached from reality.

## 12. Anti-patterns
Reject:
- pastel gradient SaaS marketing style;
- generic smiling office worker stock scene;
- low-contrast gray-on-gray headline;
- tiny title with oversized irrelevant artwork;
- more than 7 equally weighted logos;
- complex dashboard microtext;
- random arrows that do not encode a real relationship;
- fake metric panels with invented numbers;
- fake quotes or fake article excerpts;
- all-black/white when a relevant brand accent would improve recognition;
- rainbow tech glow across every object;
- excessive glassmorphism used as decoration;
- “AI = robot/brain” cliché unless the article explicitly needs it.

## 13. Default decision shorthand
When uncertain, choose:
- **bigger thesis** over prettier background;
- **one recognizable person/product** over three small ones;
- **brand-coded contrast** over generic blue tech;
- **one explanatory mechanism** over decorative particles;
- **editorial realism** over fantasy;
- **organized density** over empty minimalism;
- **sharp judgment** over neutral corporate wording.
